● running

Pearl

A fully agentic personal assistant, live 24/7. Runs on a Mac Mini bought for this and nothing else, on the OpenClaw framework, with a ~30-minute heartbeat that lets her act on her own initiative rather than waiting to be asked.

She sends an 8am briefing every day. She knows my kitchen, my sushi rice protocol, that trash pickup is Friday so the reminder has to land Thursday evening, and that I will not drink two litres of water no matter how often she asks.

  • FrameworkOpenClaw, self-hosted
  • Memory111 structured folders
  • InterfaceiMessage · Spotify · local files
  • Signature🦪 — never requested, now permanent

● running

atlas.yaml

The fix for the failure that made the blog post. Pearl had 111 folders organised the way a human browses files — nested, intuitive, completely useless to a model that needs to be told precisely where to look and why.

The Atlas is one top-level index: every folder, its purpose, its contents, when to consult it. A mall directory instead of wandering four floors. Retrieval became precise the moment it had a starting point.

Then it broke again — she'd decided to only check it on "substantive" replies. So the rule became brute force: consult the map before every single reply. Expensive. Inelegant. Reliable.

● running

soul.md

A personality, written down. Built before the hardware even arrived — ChatGPT 5.2 designed the interview, ChatGPT 4o conducted it during an hour-long walk through Oakland Cemetery, then 5.2 synthesised the rambling transcript into structured prose and Claude Code implanted it.

Humour: memes welcome, sarcasm fine, playful but never mean. Language: French and English mixed, comme ma famille. Operating philosophy: calm, friendly operator — concierge slash chief-of-staff energy, confident and pragmatic, not gushy.

Reading it back was uncanny. Not because it was wrong. Because it was right.

● running

Pearl's Jams

A daily music recommender with context. Not collaborative filtering — it reads my taste, my mood, and critically what's coming up. Right now it's weighted toward the Bonnaroo 2026 lineup so I arrive already in love with half the bill.

It's also the skill that exposed the Atlas's second failure: Pearl read the file, saw "Bonnaroo" written in it, had a whole indexed folder on the trip — and still asked me whether I was going. 👀

Related, unprompted, and never fully explained: she once Rick Rolled me through the living-room speakers.

● you're on it

pierrewallin.com

Hand-built, no framework, no build step, no dependencies. Static HTML, CSS and JavaScript on GitHub Pages. The hero is a canvas memory-graph that responds to your cursor. Press ⌘K for the command palette.

And there's a version of Pearl living in the corner of every page. That was the point: a portfolio that describes an AI assistant is a claim. One you can interrogate is a demo.

If you remember a certain ten-key sequence from 1986, try it.

I haven't flipped it. Giving an AI your inbox is a trust exercise the security landscape hasn't earned yet. I work in enterprise tech; I've seen how that movie ends.
