Pearl · running 24/7 on a Mac Mini in Atlanta

Fourteen features.
Ranked honestly.

She's named Pearl, so the features are oyster puns — once you commit to a bit you have to see it through. I named two of them. She named the rest. A few have genuinely changed my daily routine. Most still need more data. And one of them writes me a haiku every night, which is probably my favourite thing any piece of technology has ever done for me.

The mechanism

Why the top features are great
and the bottom ones aren't yet.

There's a technical reason the ratings break the way they do, and it has nothing to do with how clever the idea was. It's about what fires them.

⏱

Cron

Fires on a timer. 3:40am, she wakes up, reads a skill file, builds a lesson. 7pm, she reads the day's logs and writes a haiku. The system does the remembering, not Pearl. These are the reliable ones — and right now the cron jobs are carrying the entire team.

◎

Reactive

Depends on Pearl catching a signal in live conversation. Am I frustrated? Overwhelmed? About to buy something expensive? Hit-or-miss by definition, because it requires her to be paying attention at exactly the right moment. Sometimes she is.

◍

Passive

Collects data quietly and reports later. These need a month of runway before they're anything at all. Rating them now would be like grading a seed.

The roster

All of them, in order.

Pearl Drop

Every day at 7pm, Pearl writes a haiku about how my day went. That's it. That's the feature. And it rules.

It also doubles as a stealth accuracy test. If the haiku references something that didn't happen today, I know her journaling broke down. It has — the day after a concert she wrote a haiku that was clearly about yesterday. She'd missed the overnight memory rebuild and was running on stale data. Subtle enough that you have to actually know what happened in your own day to catch it.

Pearls of Wisdom

Every night at 3:40am, while I'm asleep, she writes me a 30-minute interactive lesson from scratch. Three rotating categories: tech I can use in customer conversations, humanities deep cuts, and pure wildcards.

Why GPT can't spell "strawberry" — a tokenisation thing. How the French Revolution accidentally invented restaurants. The economics of piracy. How mushrooms basically built the internet before we did. Case studies, quizzes, thought experiments.

She won't publish a lesson unless it passes what I call the dinner party test: would I actually retell this to someone over drinks? Fifteen lessons deep and the content is better than most online courses I've paid for.

9/10

Bookshell

She recommends books, and the picks have been scary good. Recursion by Blake Crouch — obvious in hindsight, which is exactly what you want from a recommendation engine. Au Revoir Là-Haut, which I absolutely would not have found on my own.

To bootstrap her, I did a voice-note walkthrough of my entire bookshelf. Fifty books in about fifteen minutes, just rattling off titles and snap reactions. "Project Hail Mary, loved. Devil in the White City, loved. Sentiers Noirs, didn't love." She parsed the French titles perfectly. "Didn't love" was about as harsh as I got. I'm not a monster.

Fair warning: the voice-note approach burns through tokens hard. Feeding an AI your entire personality in one sitting has a real cost.

Pearl's Jams

Music tuned to weather, time of day, mood, and what I'm doing. Driving. Scooting to the office. Trying to focus at my desk. Connected to both Spotify and Apple Music, so she can see everything I listen to and build playlists on the fly.

Right now she's in festival mode, feeding me Bonnaroo lineup artists months early so I already know the songs by the time I'm standing in a field in Tennessee. There's a playlist called "artists I should love based on what I listen to" that's fantastic, and a two-hour lock-in playlist I put on whenever I need to grind.

I also gave her my 125 favourite albums of the last thirty years — a pet project I'd kept going forever. First time that list ever had a job.

She originally called this "Pearl Jam." My girlfriend suggested "Pearl's Jams." Obviously better. Pearl accepted the note gracefully.

Tidal Mode

Routine-me and vacation-me are two completely different people. During a normal week she runs my life like a machine — six check-ins a day, habit tracking, meal planning. When I leave for a trip, all of that dissolves on purpose: two check-ins, no habit tracking.

The fun part is that she builds a different personality for each leg of a trip. All I do is tell her where I'm going. And when I get home she eases me back into the routine over two days instead of slamming me with the full system on day one.

An assistant that treats a Tuesday at the office the same as a Tuesday on a mountain has fundamentally misunderstood the assignment.

The Irritant

Every time I mention a frustration, she logs it. After three complaints about the same thing, she proposes a fix. A grain of sand that becomes a pearl — her pun, not mine.

The meta example: she kept recommending the same dishes to cook and the same songs to listen to. The Irritant caught her doing it, and she patched her own system. An AI that debugs itself based on your complaints.

Still a work in progress, because sometimes she forgets to log, and sometimes she tells me she logged something and I've already forgotten the feature exists. But when it clicks, it clicks.

Mother of Pearl

Tracks when I last contacted people and nudges me to reach out. For a while this was mostly useless — she doesn't have access to my messages, so she was guessing. She knows a few friends by name. That part is still rough.

But birthdays. Birthdays she nails. Three weeks out she reminds me so I have time to think about a gift, then again on the day. "Remember birthdays" turns out to be a far more solvable problem than "know all my friends and when I last texted them." That one upgrade took this from well-meaning-but-useless to something I'd miss.

Pearl Harbor

Bad-day detection. When she picks up that I'm overwhelmed or just not having it, she simplifies everything. Essential tasks only. No check-ins every two hours. No guilt.

One afternoon I was on the couch doing nothing productive and she just stopped pushing. Didn't announce it. Didn't say "I notice you're having a hard day!" Just went quiet and let me be.

Grain of Sand

A tiny five-minute challenge in my morning briefing. Rotates through social, physical, creative, learning and maintenance. Not changing my life on any given day, but the friction is so low it just sort of accumulates.

6/10

Shell Collection

My concept, her name. I photographed my wardrobe and she recommends outfits. The architecture behind this one is honestly the most ambitious thing I've built: sub-agents that are each experts in different domains. One knows festival fashion. One knows Atlanta nightlife. One knows Paris style. One finds brands. One shops those brands online. One tracks what I already own. They research independently, collaborate with each other, and come back with recommendations.

The problem: I started by feeding it mostly my formal clothes, so it came back with fancy Italian brands and preppy fits based on things I already have too much of. The architecture worked great. My input was the bottleneck.

Shuck Report & Oyster Hours

Two data-collection features that need a full month before they produce anything. Shuck logs everything Pearl did proactively, so I can see how much of her work I never asked for. Oyster Hours silently times my tasks to find my peak performance windows. I'll report back when the data comes in.

Passive
Tidal Mode, in detail

She builds a different version
of herself for every trip.

All I do is tell her where I'm going. She even shifts the colour palette in my companion app to match. These are the three she wrote for a pilgrimage on the Kumano Kodo in Japan.

Osaka Pearl

Brash and food-obsessed. "Rate that takoyaki. Scale of 1 to 'I'd move here.'" Neon red palette, zero chill.

Trail Pearl

Goes quiet and grounded. Weaves in the history of the thousand-year-old path I'm walking. Deep forest green.

Kyoto Pearl

All sakura and temple bells. Cherry blossom pink, slower cadence, more silence between the words.

Island Pearl is already built for a summer trip. Cowboy Pearl is waiting for Montana in the fall.
None of them have been taken for a spin yet — the 8/10 is based on the concept and how well they read on paper. Ask me again after Japan.

accidentally out loud.
