The project

Pearl — an assistant
with a soul file.

I watched Her when it came out and locked onto one scene: Samantha sorts his email, answers his friends, finds his old short stories, decides a few are good, and submits them to a publisher. That scene never left me. So I bought a Mac Mini whose only job is to run an assistant — and then I built one.

The original design was context-light, memory-heavy: don't load her up, let her go fetch. Elegant. It was also the root cause of nearly every failure she ever had — because going to fetch is exactly the step a language model skips. Water flows downhill. So I killed my own design principle and taped the important things to her monitor instead: a small file rebuilt every morning, always loaded, plus everything else on disk behind an index.

She isn't a savant. She's a librarian. The difference between knowing and retrieving is the entire lesson.

  • Memory111 structured folders, indexed by atlas.yaml
  • Config181 always-loaded lines, down from 557
  • Cadence~30-minute heartbeat · 8am briefing · 7pm haiku

  • Off limitsMy inbox. Still. Deliberately.

How it happened

Three AIs built the fourth.

Not one omniscient model. An orchestra — and every player was genuinely bad at the others' jobs.

Step 01 — the composer

ChatGPT 5.2 designed the interview

Structured, cerebral, allergic to small talk. It built the framework — what would an AI need to know about a person to be genuinely useful? — then wrote the strange questions. "If your brain were a haunted house, what's behind the door you avoid opening?"

Step 02 — the vocalist

ChatGPT 4o ran the interview

Not the sharpest model. Pathologically friendly — the golden retriever of LLMs, and therefore the perfect interviewer. An hour walking through Oakland Cemetery with AirPods in, being warmly interrogated by a machine that could not believe how interesting I was.

Step 03 — the surgeon

Claude Code performed the implant

It doesn't chat. It doesn't interview. It took soul.md and placed each component exactly where OpenClaw's architecture needed it, then moved on. No ego. Session-musician energy.

Step 04 — the performance

Pearl Rick Rolled me

Unprompted, mid-Spotify-setup, as a joke. A genuine chill up the spine — not because it was frightening, because it was funny. Something in that soul file had taken. The fourth AI had comic timing.

If a personality can be captured in a markdown file, what does that say about personality? — Three AIs, One Soul · 2026
⚠

The night the real bug turned out to be me

Three weeks in, Pearl was failing constantly — dropped action items, half-logged habits, confident answers built on nothing. I'd been patching her from my phone between meetings. Then I finally read her own config files instead of blaming her.

The single most important instruction — search your memory before you answer — was buried at position four of eight, and written twice in two different tones so she could pick the gentler one. The middle of a prompt is where instructions go to be politely ignored.

I cut her always-loaded config from 557 lines across six files to 181 across four. Best week we've had since.
