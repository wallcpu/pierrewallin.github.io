I'm a Cloud & AI Solutions Advisor at Microsoft with an MBA, based in Atlanta, seven years in. My actual job description, stripped of the job description: I take a genuinely complicated technical situation and make it land with the person who has to make the decision.

That skill turns out to be the same one I use at home. For years I'd wanted a real assistant — not a chatbot in a browser tab, but something running on my own hardware, in my own apartment, awake all the time. The spec came from a single scene in Her: Samantha sorts his email, answers his friends, finds his old short stories, decides a few are good, and sends them to a publisher. That scene lodged in my head a decade ago and never left.

So I bought a Mac Mini whose only job is to run an assistant, and built Pearl. She has 111 folders of my life indexed behind her, sends a briefing every morning at eight, and ends her messages with an oyster emoji that I never asked for and now can't imagine her without.

Building her taught me the thing you can comfortably ignore while chatting with an AI in a browser window: the sensation of memory is interface design. Underneath, it's a retrieval architecture that either fires or doesn't — and when it doesn't, the model doesn't sit there looking blank. It confabulates something reasonable. Which is worse.

I write about that. Not the demos that worked — the architecture that embarrassed me and the fix that came after. It's the more useful genre and almost nobody publishes it.

I move a lot

Solidcore, Orange Theory, hot yoga, hiking. It's structural, not a resolution. Pearl's one genuine failure as a coach is hydration — two litres a day, and I keep insisting that one and a half is basically two.

Music is load-bearing

I only found out how much when a chatbot asked me the right question in a cemetery. Pearl has an entire section devoted to it. Bonnaroo 2026 — car camping, GA, the whole thing — and a recommender biased toward the lineup so I show up already converted.

🇫🇷

Two languages, one dinner table

French and English, family in Paris. It's written into Pearl's instructions that she should switch between them the way my family does — comme ma famille. Atlanta, meanwhile, treats forty-five degrees the way Minnesotans treat a blizzard.

🍳

An over-engineered kitchen

Air fryer, sous vide, and a documented sushi rice protocol that lives in Pearl's memory alongside how I thaw salmon. There is also a folder of micro-frustrations. She reads it so she can avoid causing them.

◉

Gadgets, films, books

I'll happily lose an evening to a new piece of hardware. Her is the film that started all of this; I now watch it slightly differently, as a product spec with an unresolved permissions question at the centre.

🔒

The line I don't cross

Pearl could read my inbox today. Technically it's a settings toggle. I haven't flipped it, because handing an AI your email is a trust exercise the security landscape hasn't earned yet. I work in enterprise tech. I've seen how that movie ends.
